Spoken English

Learn useful phrases, not dictionary fragments

In conversation, we retrieve complete pieces of meaning: agreeing, buying time, softening a refusal, asking for clarification or changing the subject.

Why a phrase is more useful than one word

A translation rarely tells you which words naturally go together or when an expression is appropriate. A short phrase stores meaning, grammar, word order and communicative purpose together.

A good flashcard unit

is short enough to recall quickly and specific enough to make the situation clear.

Which phrases are worth learning

  • Functional phrases help you agree, clarify, object or ask for time.
  • Situational phrases appear during travel, work, daily life or messaging.
  • Personal phrases express things you genuinely say often.
  • Register-aware phrases are clearly marked as neutral, informal or rude.

You do not need to save every line from a series. If you do not need the phrase or understand its tone, it will only enlarge the queue.

How to structure the cards

DirectionWhat it testsExample task

If one expression has several substantially different meanings, create separate cards with context instead of placing five translations in one answer.

A useful practice cycle

  1. Read the situation or Russian prompt.
  2. Say the English version aloud before revealing the answer.
  3. Compare both meaning and wording.
  4. Rate how easily you recalled it.
  5. Create one more reply using the same pattern.

Cards maintain access to a phrase in memory. Transfer to conversation still requires actual use.
